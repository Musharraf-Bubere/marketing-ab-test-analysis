## 2. Hypothesis Formulation & Plain-English Explanations

### Hypothesis Setup
* **Null Hypothesis ($H_0$)**: $p_{\text{ad}} = p_{\text{psa}}$  
  *Plain English*: The commercial ad has no true effect on customer conversion. Any observed difference between the 2.55% treatment rate and the 1.79% control rate is purely random sampling variation.
* **Alternative Hypothesis ($H_1$)**: $p_{\text{ad}} \ne p_{\text{psa}}$  
  *Plain English*: The commercial ad changes conversion behavior. The treatment conversion rate is genuinely different from the control conversion rate.

---

### Key Statistical Concepts Explained in Business Terms

#### 1. What does the $p$-value ($1.71 \times 10^{-13}$) mean?
* **Precise Definition**: Assuming the null hypothesis is true (the ad has zero real effect), the probability of observing a difference in conversion rates as extreme as or more extreme than $+0.77$ percentage points due to random sampling variation alone is $1.71 \times 10^{-13}$.
* **Business Translation**: This probability is less than **1 in 5 trillion**. While a $p$-value is not "the probability that the null hypothesis is true," it provides overwhelming empirical evidence against $H_0$.

#### 2. What does the 95% Confidence Interval ($[+0.60\%\text{ pts}, +0.94\%\text{ pts}]$) mean?
* **Precise Definition**: If this experiment were replicated repeatedly under identical conditions and independent samples, 95% of the calculated confidence intervals would contain the true population lift.
* **Business Translation**: It establishes a plausible range for the true effect rather than a point estimate. While not an assurance of future performance (which depends on stable market and seasonal conditions), $+0.60$ percentage points represents a conservative lower boundary for planning.

#### 3. Statistical Significance vs. Practical (Economic) Significance
* **The Distinction**: Cohen's $h = 0.0530$ is categorized as a "small" effect size by textbook benchmarks.
* **The Business Truth**: In high-volume operations, small percentage shifts scale into massive volume (+4,343 incremental conversions across 565k users). However, **statistical significance does not automatically equal commercial profitability**. True business success depends on whether the revenue generated by those 4,343 incremental conversions exceeds the ad spend required to serve 14 million impressions—a calculation modeled in Phase 6.

#### 4. Statistical Power & The Post-Hoc Power Caveat
* **Sample Size Capacity**: With over 588,000 total observations, the test had extraordinary prospective power to detect minute differences.
* **Methodological Caution**: Post-hoc (retrospective) power calculated from observed effect sizes is mathematically tethered to the $p$-value and does not imply an absence of statistical uncertainty. However, the massive sample size ensures that this test was not underpowered.

## 3. Segment Significance & The Multiple Comparisons Problem

When we slice the experiment by day of the week, we perform 7 separate hypothesis tests. If an analyst tests 7 hypotheses at $\alpha = 0.05$, the probability of at least one false positive increases:
$$\text{Family-Wise Error Rate} = 1 - (1 - 0.05)^7 \approx 30.2\%$$

To prevent false discoveries, we applied the **Bonferroni correction** (adjusted significance threshold: $\alpha_{\text{adjusted}} = 0.05 / 7 = \mathbf{0.00714}$):

## 4. Critical Analytical Caveats (What Senior Analysts Flag)

1. **The Ad Exposure / Reverse-Causality Confound**:
   As established in Phase 2 and Phase 3, ad impressions are not randomly assigned. High-intent customers visit more pages and naturally get served more ads. We must not claim that forcing 50 ads onto every customer creates a 11.6% conversion rate.
2. **Novelty & Satiation Effects**:
   When a new ad campaign launches, existing users may click simply out of curiosity (novelty effect). Over time, repeated exposure to the exact same creative causes banner blindness and ad fatigue. A long-term rollout requires periodic creative refreshes.
3. **Temporal Aggregation**:
   The dataset records `most_ads_day` and `most_ads_hour`, rather than an event-level timestamp sequence showing exactly when each ad was viewed relative to when the purchase occurred. Our timing analysis identifies user activity clusters rather than instantaneous reaction times.
